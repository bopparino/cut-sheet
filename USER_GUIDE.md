# Cut Sheet: How To Use It

This is a plain guide to the Cut Sheet app. It is written for someone who is comfortable with paperwork but not with computers. Take your time. Nothing here can break the program, and anything you remove can be brought back.

If you get stuck, the short version is this: you find a cut sheet or make a new one, you fill in the boxes, you save, and then you print it for the shop. Everything else is detail.

---

## 1. What this app is

For years the shop used a paper cut sheet, and before that an old Access database. This app does the same job on the computer. It holds every cut sheet, it keeps them organized by builder, and it prints the same tickets the shop already knows, on the same paper sizes.

A "cut sheet" is one house. It lists what goes where: the duct, the fittings, the pipe, the registers, the plenum, all of it. One house can have more than one cut sheet (for example a second floor or a separate zone), and that is completely normal.

You do not install anything. It is a website. You open it in whatever web browser is already on your computer, the same way you open your email or a news page. It does not matter which browser you use.

**Tip:** open the app once, then save it as a bookmark or favorite so you can get back to it with one click. Ask whoever set this up for the web address if you do not have it yet.

---

## 2. The three things you will do most

Almost everything you do is one of these three:

1. **Find a cut sheet** that already exists, so you can look at it or change it.
2. **Make a new cut sheet** for a house that does not have one yet.
3. **Print a cut sheet** so the shop can build it.

The rest of this guide walks through each one.

---

## 3. Getting around: the main screen

When you open the app you land on the **Browse** screen. This is home base. On the left side you will always see a short menu:

- **Browse.** Home. Your recent cut sheets and the full list of builders.
- **Search.** A place to look up any cut sheet by typing.
- **New cutsheet.** Starts a fresh, blank cut sheet.
- **Trash.** Where removed cut sheets wait, in case you need one back.

You can click these at any time to move around. You will not lose your place in a dangerous way, but remember: if you are in the middle of typing on a cut sheet, save first (more on saving later).

### The Browse screen, top to bottom

- At the very top it tells you how many builders and how many cut sheets are in the system.
- **Recent** shows the last handful of cut sheets that were opened or changed. If you were just working on something, it is probably right here. Click it to open it.
- **Builders** is the big list. Builders are sorted A to Z, the same way names are in a phone book. Click a builder to open it up, and you will see all of that builder's cut sheets listed underneath. Click any cut sheet in that list to open it.

That is the important part to remember: **click a builder to open it, then click the cut sheet you want.**

---

## 4. Finding a cut sheet

You have two ways to find something.

### The quick way: the search box

At the top right of the Browse screen there is a search box. Click it, type anything you remember, and press Enter. It will take you to the Search screen with your results.

### The full way: the Search screen

Click **Search** on the left menu. You get one box. Type into it and it looks everywhere at once. You can search by any of these, and you do not have to know which one you are typing:

- the cut sheet name or the house model (like MADISON or HAYDEN)
- the builder
- the project or community
- the lot number
- the property number (prop number)
- the project code, the foreman, the block, the section, the zone

So if you only remember "lot 43" or "Hayden" or a prop number, type that. Whatever matches will show up in the list. Click a result to open it.

There are also two date boxes, **Delivery from** and **Delivery to**. Use these if you want to narrow the list to houses being delivered in a certain window. Leave them blank to ignore them. The **Clear** button empties everything and starts over.

**Note:** it is normal to see several cut sheets that share a lot number. The shop does more than one sheet per house, so that is expected, not an error.

## 5. Making a new cut sheet

1. Click **New cutsheet** on the left.
2. At the top, give it a name. Usually this is the house type or model. This name is what you will see later in the lists, so make it something you will recognize.
3. Fill in the **header** boxes with whatever job information you have: builder, project, house type, lot, block, section, foreman, project code, option, prop number, zone, and the dates.
4. When you start typing in the **Builder** box, the app suggests builders you have used before. If you see the right one, click it instead of typing the whole thing. This keeps builder names spelled the same way every time, which matters, because that is how cut sheets get grouped together.
5. You do not have to fill in everything now. Put in what you know.
6. Click **Create** in the top right.

The app then opens your new cut sheet, ready to fill in.

---

## 6. Filling out a cut sheet

When you open a cut sheet you see two large forms, one above the other. They are made to look like the paper cut sheet you already know:

- The first one is the **Shop Cut Sheet** (60 inch duct, custom duct, end caps, plenum, and so on).
- The second one is the **Cut Sheet** (boots, flex, fans, wall caps, pipe, registers, and so on).

### The header

At the top of the first form is the header, with all the job information: builder, lot, block, prop number, zone, foreman, dates, and the rest. The **Builder** box here also suggests builders you have used before, just like on the new cut sheet screen. Fill in whatever needs updating.

### Typing quantities

Most of the form is boxes next to item names. To order some of an item, click its box and type the number you need. To order none of it, leave the box empty. That is the whole idea: **a number means you want that many, an empty box means none.**

### Plenum Package

Near the top you will see Plenum Package with three choices: **Small**, **Large**, and **None**. Click the one you want. If the house has no plenum package, you can pick None, or you can leave all three unpicked. A brand new cut sheet starts with nothing picked, which is on purpose.

### The name

The box at the very top left, above everything, is the cut sheet's name. Change it any time. It is what shows up in your lists.

### Saving your work (important)

When you finish, or any time you want to lock in your changes, click the black **Save** button in the top right corner.

Here is the part to remember: **nothing is saved until you click Save.** If you type a bunch of numbers and then leave the page without clicking Save, that typing is gone. It works like a paper form you have not filed yet. So when in doubt, click Save. There is no harm in saving often.

---

## 7. Fittings: pick, size, done

Below the two forms is the **Fittings** section. This replaces the old Paint routine — no more screenshotting the fittings sheet, pasting into Paint, and typing sizes onto the picture.

Here is the new routine:

1. Click **+ Add Fittings**. The whole catalog of fitting drawings opens right there.
2. Tap the drawing you need. It gets added to your list. Tap it again if the house needs another one of the same kind — a little counter on the drawing keeps track.
3. Click **Done Picking** when you have them all.
4. On each fitting in your list, fill in the quantity and the measurements (W, H, L, and so on — each fitting shows its own size boxes). There is a notes box on every line for anything extra, like "drive both ends".

It saves by itself as you type — you will see a little "Saving…" appear and disappear. No Save button needed for fittings.

When you print for the shop, every fitting you picked comes out on the fittings page: the drawing, the quantity nice and big, and the measurements in bold underneath.

### Attachments: pictures and files

The **Attachments** section still works like it always has: click **+ Add files** and pick any picture or file. Pictures land on the fittings page after the picked fittings, so hand-drawn one-offs still print for the crew. Anything that is not a picture is just kept on file with the cut sheet.

To remove a file, hover over it and click the small X.

---

## 8. Plans: the house blueprints

Right below Attachments is the **Plans** section. This is only for house plans, the blueprints.

Click **+ Add Plans**, pick the file, and it uploads. A single plan can be one page or several pages, and that is fine. When you print for the shop, the plans come out on the big 11 by 17 paper.

Plans can be PDF files or pictures (BMP, PNG, or JPG). A picture gets turned into a PDF for you the moment it uploads, so it prints on 11 by 17 like everything else.

---

## 9. Printing for the shop

This is the payoff. When a cut sheet is filled in, you send it to the shop.

At the top of the cut sheet you have two main buttons.

### Send to Shop

This is the one click that does it all. It gathers everything for that house into one PDF:

- the full **Cut Sheet**, on 8.5 by 14 paper (legal)
- the three pick **tickets** (Stock, Custom, Truck), on 8.5 by 11 paper (letter)
- the **fittings** page with your drawings, on 8.5 by 14 paper
- any **plans** you added, on 11 by 17 paper

Each part comes out on its correct paper size, all in one print job, just like the old program did. When you click Send to Shop, that PDF opens in a new tab. Then you print it the normal way (your print button, or hold Ctrl and press P on Windows, or Command and P on a Mac), and you pick the shop printer. The printer pulls each page from the right paper tray on its own.

It can take a few seconds to build, especially if there are a lot of plan pages. That is normal. A full packet can be anywhere from a handful of pages to close to twenty when the plans are large, and that is just how it is.

### Print Here

This makes the exact same PDF, but instead of opening it to print, it **downloads it to your computer**. Use this when you want to keep a copy, email it, or print it on a printer near your desk instead of the shop.

### Printing just one document

Sometimes you only need one piece. Along the top you will also see buttons for **Filled PDF**, **Stock**, **Custom**, and **Truck**. Click any of these to open just that single document by itself.

---

## 10. Removing a cut sheet, and the Trash

Every cut sheet you remove goes to the **Trash** (on the left menu) rather than being erased. From the Trash you can bring a cut sheet back if you removed it by mistake. This is your safety net, so you never have to worry that one wrong click destroys real work.

---

## 11. Common questions

**Do I have to save?**
Yes. Click the Save button after you make changes. Nothing is kept until you do.

**I cannot find my cut sheet.**
Use the Search screen and type anything you remember: a lot number, a prop number, the builder, the model name. If a builder's name is spelled two different ways, its sheets end up split into two groups, so watch the spelling and use the builder suggestions when you type.

**Which printer do I pick?**
For shop work, pick the shop printer in the print box. For a copy at your desk, use Print Here and pick your own printer.

**How many fittings can I add?**
As many as you need. The fittings page adds more pages by itself as you add more drawings.

**A page printed on the wrong size paper.**
The app sends each page at its correct size. If a page comes out wrong, the printer or its tray settings are usually the cause, not the app. Make sure the printer is loaded with letter, legal, and 11 by 17, and that it is set to choose the tray by page size.

**I see two cut sheets with the same lot.**
That is expected. The shop makes more than one sheet per house.

**Did I break something?**
Almost certainly not. You cannot hurt the data by clicking around. The worst that happens is you leave a page without saving, and even then you only lose the typing you had not saved yet.

---

## 12. A quick word list

- **Cut sheet:** one house. The full list of what the shop builds for it.
- **Ticket:** one printed pick list for a role. Stock, Custom, or Truck.
- **Fitting:** a shaped piece of duct. You pick these from the catalog and fill in the sizes.
- **Plenum:** the box on the furnace. Small, Large, or None.
- **Plans:** the house blueprints (PDF or picture files), printed on 11 by 17.
- **Packet:** everything for one house, printed together in one job.
- **Builder:** the company building the house. Cut sheets are grouped by builder.
- **Lot:** the lot number of the house in its community.
- **Prop number:** the property number for the house. It prints on every document.
- **Header:** the top of a cut sheet, with the job information.
- **Save:** the button that keeps your changes. Use it often.

---

That is the whole app. Find it or make it, fill it in, save it, print it. Everything else you will pick up by doing it a few times. When you are unsure, click Save, and remember that the Trash holds anything you remove.
